Scope of this application. This application is designed for federal R&D credit {{ 'studies' if is_study else 'feasibility studies' }} for tax years beginning in 2025 or later. It assumes domestic research and experimental (R&E) expenditures are currently deducted under IRC Section 174A rather than separately modeled as a capitalization/amortization study.

It primarily evaluates the federal Section 41 research credit. Explain this – why entity type matters

The research activity can qualify regardless of entity type, but the credit is reported and used differently.

C corporation

The corporation computes the credit on Form 6765 and claims it as a general business credit at the corporate level (generally through Form 3800). The benefit reduces the corporation's federal income-tax liability, subject to the general business credit rules.

S corporation / partnership

The entity computes the research credit on Form 6765. The income-tax credit generally passes through to shareholders or partners on Schedule K-1 and is ultimately subject to owner-level limitations. A qualified-small-business payroll-tax election, when available, is made at the entity level.

Sole proprietor / single-member LLC

The owner claims the business activity and related credit on the owner's return, generally using Form 6765 and Form 3800 (Form 3800 pulls together separate business credits such as R&D, investment and hiring incentives). The activity is commonly reported on Schedule C.

Explain this – why the app starts at 2025 (Section 174 vs. 174A)

Why 2025 and later

For domestic research or experimental (R&E) costs, new IRC Section 174A restores a current deduction for tax years beginning after December 31, 2024. Taxpayers may instead elect to capitalize and amortize domestic R&E over at least 60 months. The {{ 'app' if is_study else 'feasibility app' }} assumes the current-deduction path for domestic R&E.

What was different in 2022–2024

Domestic specified research expenditures were generally required to be capitalized and amortized over 5 years under the prior Section 174 rules (foreign R&E over 15 years). The Section 41 credit did not disappear; the major change was the deduction/capitalization treatment that interacts with the credit.

2022–2024 balances already capitalized

Rev. Proc. 2025-28 allows a taxpayer with remaining unamortized domestic 2022–2024 Section 174 amounts to recover the remaining balance either entirely in the first tax year beginning after December 31, 2024, or ratably over that year and the next. This transition is separate from whether the taxpayer claimed the Section 41 credit.

Expired relief: eligible small-business taxpayers had special OBBBA relief to retroactively apply Section 174A to 2022–2024 through amended returns / AARs. That window generally closed on the earlier of the normal refund-claim deadline or July 6, 2026, and is no longer available.

Form 6765 Section G (business components)

Optional for tax years beginning before 2026; generally required for tax years beginning after 2025. Section G is not required if all three are true: (1) total QREs are $1.5 million or less at the controlled-group level; (2) average annual gross receipts for the prior 3 tax years are $50 million or less; and (3) the credit is reported on an original return.

Explain this – why the year R&D began matters (ASC and Regular credit)

The ASC calculation method generally looks to the prior three years of QREs. If the taxpayer had no QREs in any one of the prior three years, the special 6% ASC rule may apply. The Regular Credit start-up rules also depend on the taxpayer's early years with QREs, not simply the company's age.

Normally, ASC is calculated as 14% × (current-year QREs − 50% of the average QREs for the prior 3 years). But if any one of those three prior years had zero QREs, the normal ASC formula is replaced by 6% × current-year QREs.

Controlled-group and common-control rules can require related businesses to be treated together when computing the research credit and when testing payroll-credit gross receipts.

Explain this – what filing status changes (and what it doesn't)

Filing the return does not automatically eliminate the research credit. It changes which elections are still available and whether the claim is made on an original return, an amended return, or (for a BBA partnership) an Administrative Adjustment Request (AAR).

Return not yet filed

The app determines whether the original return can still be timely filed based on the entity, tax year-end and extension. A timely original return can still make elections that require one – including the Section 280C reduced-credit election and, if the taxpayer qualifies, the QSB payroll-tax election.

Return already filed

A research credit may still be claimed or increased on an amended return if the normal refund statute is open and the special research-credit refund-claim requirements are satisfied. For tax years beginning before 2026, Section G is generally optional on an original return, but amended research-credit claims have additional business-component information requirements.

280C: must be made on the original timely filed return (including extensions) and cannot ordinarily be first made or changed on an amended return. The special OBBBA late-280C relief for 2022–2024 ended no later than July 6, 2026.

Payroll election: must be made on or before the due date of the originally filed income-tax return, including extensions. It cannot be added later simply by amending the return.

ASC on an amended return: if no research credit was previously claimed for the year, the ASC election may generally be made on an amended return. If a credit was already claimed using the Regular method, the method cannot simply be switched to ASC by amendment.
