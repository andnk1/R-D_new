Explain this – current-year gross receipts

Enter the actual gross-receipts amount for the study year. The app uses the amount in payroll eligibility and, when applicable, the credit calculation.

Gross receipts include items such as interest, dividends, rents, royalties, and other income depending on the taxpayer.

For the QSB payroll-tax election, current-year gross receipts must be less than $5 million. Exactly $5 million does not satisfy the "less than $5 million" test.

What happens above $5 million: the taxpayer is not eligible for the QSB payroll-tax election for that year.

Common confusion: do not mix the $5 million QSB payroll threshold with other gross-receipts tests. The Section 174A small-business transition uses the Section 448(c) test (for 2025, generally $31 million average annual gross receipts, subject to the detailed rules), and the "eligible small business" AMT rule for the research credit uses a different threshold.

Who is a "startup"?

"Startup" here is a special tax definition – not necessarily a newly formed company. A company falls under the Regular R&D credit startup rules if either:

  • its first tax year with both gross receipts and QREs began after 1983; or
  • it had both gross receipts and QREs in fewer than three tax years during 1984–1988.

Practically: a company formed in 2000, 2015, or 2025 generally falls under these rules, even if it is now an established business.

Then count its years with QREs to determine the fixed-base percentage:

  • First five QRE years: 3%.
  • Sixth QRE year onward: use the historical formulas in the fixed-base table above.
It does not restart at 3% simply because this is the company's first year claiming the credit. Prior qualifying research years count.
