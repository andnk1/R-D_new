Explain this – the Qualified Small Business (payroll tax) receipts test

The first year with gross receipts is a core part of the Qualified Small Business (QSB) payroll-tax credit test. A QSB generally must have current-year gross receipts of less than $5 million and no gross receipts for any tax year before the five-tax-year period ending with the credit year.

For a 2025 credit year, the five-year period is 2021–2025. Gross receipts before 2021 generally prevent QSB payroll status. For a 2026 credit year, gross receipts before 2022 generally prevent QSB payroll status.

No earlier gross receipts? That does not hurt the test – a pre-revenue startup can satisfy the receipt-history requirement. The app evaluates the dates automatically.

Pitfalls: a financing round is not automatically gross receipts – equity contributions generally are not, while service revenue, grants, token sales, interest and other receipts may need analysis. Predecessors and controlled / common-control entities can also affect the test.

Explain this – current-year gross receipts

Enter the actual gross-receipts amount for the study year. The app uses the amount in payroll eligibility and, when applicable, the credit calculation.

Gross receipts include items such as interest, dividends, rents, royalties, and other income depending on the taxpayer.

For the QSB payroll-tax election, current-year gross receipts must be less than $5 million. Exactly $5 million does not satisfy the "less than $5 million" test.

What happens above $5 million: the taxpayer is not eligible for the QSB payroll-tax election for that year.

Common confusion: do not mix the $5 million QSB payroll threshold with other gross-receipts tests. The Section 174A small-business transition uses the Section 448(c) test (for 2025, generally $31 million average annual gross receipts, subject to the detailed rules), and the "eligible small business" AMT rule for the research credit uses a different threshold.

Explain this – shortcut for calculations

Company existed but had neither qualifying R&D expenses nor gross receipts: The regular method generally gives 10% of current QREs because the base is zero and the 50% limitation applies (with the §280C reduced-credit election: current QREs × 7.9%). ASC gives 6%. Regular generally produces the larger credit.

Company existed and had sales, but no prior qualifying R&D expenses: The regular method generally uses a 3% fixed-base percentage multiplied by average gross receipts for the preceding four years. That base reduces the expenses eligible for the credit. ASC gives 6% of current QREs. Compare both methods; substantial prior sales may make ASC more favorable.

Company existed and had qualifying R&D expenses, but never claimed the credit: Prior QREs still count. Compare the regular method, which requires historical gross receipts and the applicable fixed-base percentage, with ASC. ASC uses the 14% formula if there were QREs in all three prior years; otherwise, it uses 6% of current QREs.
